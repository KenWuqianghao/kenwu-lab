Harvard's DASCH project scanned a century of glass sky plates. We searched them for Pluto before its 1930 discovery. On the plates from J. H. Metcalf's observatory in Taunton, Massachusetts (series "ma", 1905 to 1913), the times are wrong.

Peter Graham published the Pluto image on plate MA 731 first ([DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24376800](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24376800)). He fitted the plate time to the trail of the asteroid (179) Klytaemnestra, 348 minutes before the DASCH time. He gave no cause for the offset. Our position on ma00731 agrees with his to 0.19". Our Zenodo record ([DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24508622](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24508622)) adds the cause of the time error and two more plates.

## What we found

Metcalf logged local mean time counted from noon. DASCH read these values as local sidereal time. The rule has no free parameter. It shifts the time by about +6 h in December.

- It fits 47 of 54 Minor Planet Center times of Metcalf's asteroids. The DASCH times fit 8.
- It fits 34 of 36 times that Metcalf printed in Astronomische Nachrichten. The DASCH times fit 0.
- Daylight exposures drop from 14 of 60 ma plates to 1 of 60.
- On all 4 Pluto plates, a known asteroid trail is at the corrected time (z +41 to +56). It is absent at the DASCH time (z −0.6 to +0.4, control maximum +2.5).

![Four plate cutouts, each with an asteroid trail in a green circle and an empty red circle](assets/pluto-metcalf-clock/stamps.png)

Each asteroid trail is at the corrected time (green), not at the DASCH time (red).

At the corrected times, a faint source is at Pluto's position on ma00731 (z +7.5) and ma00733 (z +5.2), weakly on ma00729 (z +2.0), all December 1908. It is also on ma00937 (z +6.5, 12 December 1909), which is outside Graham's search window.

## How it was checked

Two independent audits support the result. The first found no known asteroid brighter than V 21 within 1' and no known comet within 2°. The source moves at Pluto's rate. None of 1,045 control tracks reached it. The asteroid-trail test and the Astronomische Nachrichten search came later and have no audit.

## Limits

Nobody has read Metcalf's logbooks, so the cause is shown from data only. The Pluto images are candidates. An expert must examine the glass plates.
